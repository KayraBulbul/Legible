How to Boil an Egg

Boiling an egg sounds like the simplest thing in the kitchen, yet it is one of the most commonly botched techniques among home cooks. The difference between a perfectly set yolk and a chalky, sulfurous-smelling disappointment comes down to a handful of variables: starting temperature, egg size, altitude, and exactly how long the egg spends in the water. Get those right and you will never second-guess a boiled egg again.

What You Need

  • Eggs, straight from the fridge
  • A saucepan large enough to hold them in a single layer
  • A slotted spoon
  • A bowl of ice water

Steps

  1. Place the eggs in the saucepan and cover them with cold water by about an inch.
  2. Set the pan over high heat and bring the water to a full rolling boil.
  3. As soon as it boils, turn off the heat, cover the pan, and let the eggs sit in the hot water. Six minutes gives a soft, jammy yolk; ten minutes gives a fully set yolk with no chalky ring.
  4. While the eggs sit, fill a bowl with ice water.
  5. Using the slotted spoon, transfer the eggs immediately into the ice water and let them cool for at least five minutes before peeling.

Why the Ice Bath Matters

Plunging the eggs into ice water does two things: it stops the cooking process instantly, so a perfectly timed yolk does not keep firming up from residual heat, and it shrinks the egg slightly inside its shell, which makes peeling far easier and reduces the odds of tearing the white.

Common Mistakes

Starting the timer before the water actually boils is the single biggest source of inconsistent results. Skipping the ice bath is the second most common mistake, since the yolk keeps cooking from residual heat and can turn grey-green at the edges even after the egg is out of the pan.
